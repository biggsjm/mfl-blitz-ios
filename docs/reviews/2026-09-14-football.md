# Independent football and fantasy-football review

September 14, 2026. AI review from a football/fantasy-football product perspective; no claim of professional credentials. This first pass was completed without reading the other reviewers' findings. Sources were inspected read-only; no live feed, server, notification, simulator, or device actions were performed.

The app already covers the important management jobs. The next investment should make game-day meaning and lineup readiness easier to see, before adding more destinations or prediction features.

## Evidence and scope

Current source is the primary evidence. `docs/product-brief.md` explains intent but its build-28 date and some background-data statements are out of date, so it was not treated as proof of current behavior. Visual evidence includes the user's September 14 timeline photo, build-56 stat screenshots, build-57 scoring/alert/Live Activity fixtures, and build-60 search screenshots. Fixture names and artificial statistics are not evidence of a live scoring error.

## Prioritized findings

### 1. P1 — Separate lineup readiness from lineup legality

**Observed in source:** `AppModel.swift:921` validates starter count and position limits. `LineupView.swift:630` can show “Submitted” after that validation, while health information lives in individual rows (`:680`) and bye context in availability captions. There is no single readiness summary of known Out/IR/Inactive/bye starters. This is not a claim that an illegal lineup can be submitted.

**Consequence:** A full, saved lineup can still contain an avoidable zero. A manager scanning the top of the screen can mistake successful submission for game-day readiness.

**Change:** Add one compact “2 starters need attention” row above the starting lineup, expanding to named problems with the existing Replace action. Keep Questionable distinct from Out, and stale/unknown availability distinct from healthy. Never silently change starters or prevent a permitted intentional selection.

**Acceptance:** Fixtures for a complete healthy lineup, Out starter, bye starter, Questionable starter, locked starter, missing injury data, and unsaved replacement. Each known problem leads to eligible replacements; no “ready” claim from unavailable data. Preserve current server readback and draft recovery.

### 2. P1 — Make the displayed fantasy score explainable where it is scanned

**Observed visually and in source:** The receiving-TD fix is present: the compact row includes “2 rec · 23 rec yd · 1 rec TD,” and the detail breakdown correctly displays 2 + 1 + 6 = 9 for the fixture league. However, `MatchupDetailView.swift:552` uses the generic provider `summary`, while league-rule reconciliation only appears in the separate player detail (`ScoringBreakdownView.swift:17–49`). `NFLScoringFeed.swift:30–96` includes provider stats regardless of whether the league awards points for them. Unsupported or missing contributions remain explicitly unknown in `ScoringBreakdown.swift`, which is correct.

**Consequence:** Users still need to remember scoring thresholds and inspect another screen to learn why displayed stats do not reconcile. Unknown data can look like a complete compact stat line.

**Change:** Reuse the league scoring rules to prioritize scoring-relevant compact stats and expose a short “Points detail” affordance next to the actual score. When a verified mismatch exists, show “Stats incomplete” or a small discrepancy indicator; expand to the existing contribution list. Retain non-scoring football context in the full box score. Do not replace MFL totals with locally calculated totals.

**Acceptance:** Standard/PPR/threshold-yardage leagues, bonuses, a lost fumble, two-point conversions, kicker and D/ST unsupported rules, and asynchronously arriving stats. Fully supported fixtures reconcile at league precision; partial data is clearly partial; zero is not substituted for missing values. No per-player paid feed calls.

### 3. P1 — Show who can still score on the matchup page

**Observed:** The Live Activity visual has “5 playing · 1 yet to play” per team. The in-app hero renders score/projection/status but no per-side remaining context (`ScoringViews.swift:227–241`). The player comparison is grouped by lineup position and bench is correctly collapsed (`MatchupDetailView.swift:69–142`).

**Consequence:** A Monday-night matchup with a small deficit means very different things with zero, one, or three players left. The app currently makes users count rows while its lock-screen surface already answers the question.

**Change:** Reuse the existing validated playing/yet-to-play model in the hero. Add a reversible “Still playing” view or jump to the relevant starters without discarding the normal position comparison. Show next kickoff between games. Keep estimates secondary and avoid introducing an unsupported win probability.

**Acceptance:** Pregame, active, between games, all final, missing clocks, and stale scores; per-side counts exclude bench and never infer active play from kickoff time alone. Returning to All preserves comparison order and scroll context.

### 4. P2 — Make the timeline a history of football-relevant changes

**Observed in the user's photo:** Repeated availability-gap rows dominate the page and the technical introduction/footer outweigh useful content. Root is already simplifying that specific presentation; treat that as work in progress, not a future duplicate task.

**Remaining source issue:** `MatchupTimeline.swift:107` creates a gap for any interval of at least 210 seconds, without game-state context. `:110–117` stores both team totals and individual starter changes for a single check. Those are useful raw observations but not yet a concise game story.

**Consequence:** An ordinary interval between games can read like a failure, and one scoring update can appear as multiple independent events. Users looking for the play that changed their matchup instead see collection mechanics.

**Change:** Group one observation into a team score change with contributing players, mark a verified lead change, and tuck known coverage gaps between event groups. Suppress non-actionable off-hours gaps only where game state proves no play was active; keep the underlying observations. Separate corrections from scoring when evidence supports it. Never claim a touchdown or exact play order from a point delta alone.

**Acceptance:** No score changes, ordinary overnight break, real live interruption, simultaneous player changes, negative correction, local/background overlap, and relaunch. No double-counted deltas or invented plays; history retains uncertainty without overwhelming the list.

### 5. P2 — Make alert coverage and limits visible without paragraphs

**Observed:** Settings correctly says “Connected for Week N” (`LineupAlerts.swift:194`) and explains weekly renewal, but it sits below long footers (`:233–240`). Registration is fixed to a week and expires after eight days; server checks are within the 30 minutes before a roster player's kickoff. “Unavailable” uses MFL's specific Out/IR/Inactive statuses; injury data is accepted up to 48 hours old (`services/live_activity/lineup_alerts.py:49–69`). The player availability view explicitly calls that feed daily (`PlayerAvailabilityView.swift:76`). No end-to-end notification delivery was tested in this review.

**Consequence:** A user can enable a toggle and expect lasting monitoring or authoritative last-minute inactivity news. The current implementation has narrower coverage than that expectation.

**Change:** Put a concise status first: “Week 2 alerts on,” “Reconnect to renew,” or “Permission needed,” with an actual coverage end/next-check detail behind disclosure. Expose it from Lineup as well as Settings. Keep the current options distinct. Consider a separate bye-starter warning using verified schedule data; do not present missing injury reports as clearance. Broader live-inactive coverage requires source validation, not just more frequent polling of a daily report.

**Acceptance:** Week rollover, expired registration, app not opened for a week, permission revoked, server unreachable, option changes, stale injuries, and duplicate checks. Test actual device delivery during an eligible window before claiming reliability. Preserve opt-in and no paid NFL requests for alerts.

### 6. P2 — Distinguish a score change from full-game statistics on the Live Activity

**Observed:** `MatchupActivityChangeTracker.swift:58` names a player only when the entire starter delta explains the team delta, a valuable safeguard. `MatchupActivityController.swift:244–246` then attaches the player's full `summary` as `statContext`. The inspected card displays a “+3.0 pts” update above a full-game receiving line with a touchdown.

**Consequence (inference):** A glance can imply those complete game totals caused the latest +3, even though they include earlier plays. The fixture's quarterback/receiving combination is test data, not a production bug finding.

**Change:** Use a short “Game totals” cue for cumulative statistics, or show an actual stat delta only when two compatible fresh observations establish it. Keep “Last change” wording and retain aggregate team changes when attribution is uncertain.

**Acceptance:** Several plays between checks, a touchdown earlier than the latest yardage change, both teams changing simultaneously, and score corrections. No last-play language unless the source identifies the play; stale full-game stats disappear or qualify independently.

## Keep, and defer

Keep MFL as the scoring and roster authority; confirmed writes and retained drafts; native five-tab structure; visible owners in search; explicit missing data; collapsed bench scoring; and the shared budgeted feed. The current league-specific threshold scoring support, exact player-ID translations, and honest live-estimate label are strengths.

Defer win probabilities, automated lineup optimization, power rankings, a new social feed, or playoff-clinch claims until their league/data assumptions are supportable. A lightweight compare-with-my-starter panel would be useful later in player research/waivers, but it ranks below the six changes above. Existing waiver round semantics, FAAB review, and exact trade-asset review should remain intact.

This report proposes product improvements and testable concerns. It does not certify live notification delivery, all league formats, paid-provider completeness, or new on-device behavior.

## Discussion addendum

After the independent passes, I read the sports-design and Apple-design reports and discussed the tradeoffs directly with both reviewers. Both peers and I explicitly accepted this shared order:

1. Fix action-color contrast and the source-confirmed expanded Dynamic Island accessibility omission; preserve bright brand fills.
2. Make league-aware weekly player detail consistent from every entry route, with scoring completeness and game-phase hierarchy.
3. Summarize lineup readiness and acknowledged weekly alert coverage with the relevant next action.
4. Keep matchup totals and per-side remaining-player context available while scrolling, preserving stable default player order.
5. Group timeline observations into meaningful score updates and make gap semantics game-aware. The root's current copy/disclosure cleanup is a separate, immediate improvement.
6. Reduce Live Activity duplication and distinguish cumulative game totals from the latest point delta.
7. Validate search scope/density/focus and My Team task hierarchy before further navigation polish.

I changed my first-pass ordering because the cross-entry scoring inconsistency is source-confirmed and affects an existing core task, while global action contrast affects all those tasks. I support the small accessibility fixes first, then the football workflows. Literal negative deltas such as “−2.0 pts” are safer than labeling them corrections: lost fumbles or interceptions can legitimately decrease a score. Use “Correction” only when established by the source. Known availability, legal submission, and connected alert coverage must remain separate states. Search focus, My Team scroll burden, live notification delivery, and Always On presentation still need runtime validation; they are not newly confirmed failures. This is an agreed recommendation backlog, not approval to implement every package.
